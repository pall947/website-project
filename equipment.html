Equipment

Great espresso at home starts with the right tools. This guide offers a practical overview of what you need to get started.

The heart of every espresso setup is four tools: a machine, a grinder, a scale, and a tamper. Add a milk pitcher if you plan to make lattes or cappuccinos. Everything else is a bonus you can add later.

Espresso Machine

Espresso machines come in three types:

Manual

You supply the pressure by hand, usually with a lever. Demands skill and patience, rewards both. Best for tinkerers and travel.

Semi-automatic

A pump handles the pressure; you handle the grind, dose, and timing. Real control without doing everything yourself.

Fully automatic

Grinds, tamps, and brews at the push of a button. Demands nothing, but leaves little room to learn or improve.

Semi-automatic is the standard choice for home brewers who want to learn the craft. This guide focuses on these machines.

You don't need a pro budget to start. Most beginners find excellent semi-automatic machines under €600.

Beginner Advice: Start with an entry-level semi-automatic like the De'Longhi Dedica to learn without overspending. If your budget allows and you brew daily, consider mid-range options like the Sage Bambino Plus or Rancilio Silvia for better consistency. If you want a beginner-friendly machine with a built-in grinder at a reasonable price, take a look at the Sage Barista Express.

Grinder

Your grinder matters more than your machine. Even the best espresso machine cannot fix uneven grounds.

Entry-level electric

Solid grinders like the Sage Smart Grinder Pro or Baratza Encore ESP. Good consistency for beginners learning to adjust their settings.

Mid-range electric

More precise grinders like the Eureka Mignon Specialità or Baratza Sette. Smaller adjustment steps allow fine-tuning. Ideal for daily use.

Prosumer electric

Professional grade like the Niche Zero or Mahlkönig X54. High-quality grinding mechanisms and minimal waste. For enthusiasts wanting café quality at home.

Hand grinders

Manual options like the Timemore C5 ESP or 1Zpresso J Ultra. Equally consistent at lower prices, but require effort. Perfect for small batches or a slower morning routine.

Beginner Advice: Start with an entry-level electric to learn without overspending. If you prefer hand grinding, try the Timemore C5 ESP for great results on a budget. Upgrade the grinder before replacing your machine — it impacts taste more than any other equipment.

Core Accessories

Tamper

Compresses grounds evenly in the portafilter.

Included with most entry machines

Digital Scale

Measures coffee dose and output weight precisely.

Milk Pitcher

Required for milk-based drinks. Stainless steel with tapered spout allows precise pouring.

Included with most entry machines

Nice-to-Have Accessories

Knock box

Disposes used pucks cleanly. Optional since you can rinse over a sink.

Bottomless portafilter

Shows channeling issues visually

Distribution tool

Levels grounds before tamping for even extraction
