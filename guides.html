Step-by-Step Guide to Brewing Espresso

Making espresso involves precision, patience, and practice. Follow these steps for consistent results. Master the shot before exploring milk-based drinks.

Before You Begin

Ensure your equipment is ready:

  • Espresso machine warmed up
  • Grinder cleaned and set to fine espresso grind
  • Portafilter, tamper, and scale accessible
  • Fresh coffee beans within 2-4 weeks of roast date

Making a Double Espresso Shot

Step 1 - Dose

Weigh 14-18g of coffee on your scale. Adjust based on your machine's basket size. This is your starting point.

Step 2 - Grind

Grind immediately before brewing. Espresso grounds resemble fine sand or powder. Adjust grinder finer if extraction is too fast, coarser if too slow.

Step 3 - Distribute

Tap portafilter gently or use a distribution tool to level grounds evenly. Uneven distribution causes channeling.

Step 4 - Tamp

Apply firm, even pressure with your tamper. The surface should be perfectly flat. Avoid angling the tamper. Consistent pressure matters more than force.

Step 5 - Extract

Lock portafilter into group head. Start extraction immediately. Target a 1:2 ratio (18g in → 36g out) or 1:2.5 (18g in → 45g out). Aim for 25-30 seconds. Watch the stream—should flow like warm honey, steady and golden-brown.

Step 6 - Evaluate

Observe the shot. Light golden crema on top indicates proper extraction. Pale or thin crema suggests under-extraction. Dark, bitter taste means over-extraction. Adjust grind size accordingly.

Troubleshooting

  • Shot too fast (under 25 seconds) — Grind finer or increase dose
  • Shot too slow (over 35 seconds) — Grind coarser or decrease dose
  • No crema — Beans may be stale. Check roast date.
  • Sour taste — Under-extracted. Grind finer or extend extraction time
  • Bitter taste — Over-extracted. Grind coarser or shorten extraction time
  • Uneven extraction — Improve distribution or tamping consistency
